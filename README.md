One sentence pitching a ridiculous invention that fixes a tiny everyday
annoyance: burnt toast. A house judge compares pitches two at a time on how
funny, clever and original they are.

**The pitch** (164 characters, one sentence):

> A toaster that ejects burnt toast waving a tiny white flag and slides a handwritten apology across the counter, so breakfast begins with surrender instead of smoke.

Why this pitch: the laugh comes from the invention itself — a toaster that
treats a burnt slice as a lost battle and surrenders with a flag and a
handwritten note, instead of just beeping at you. The fix is silly yet
weirdly plausible (the machine owns its mistake instead of blaming the
bread), and the payoff lands straight-faced: breakfast starts with
surrender, not smoke. It names the annoyance (burnt toast), the invention
(the apologising toaster) and its mechanism (white flag, written apology)
in one clean sentence, with no clutter and no joke bolted on at the end.

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) prints the pitch and writes the
exact same bytes to `/output/entry.txt`, the path the kit's gate scorer
reads. `entry.txt` holds the pitch exactly as submitted. The gate requires
1–220 characters, not only whitespace; this pitch is 164.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```
